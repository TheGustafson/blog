The Series

Holding the LLM Stack in Your Head

A dependency-ordered walk through the modern LLM stack, from the linear algebra under a single attention head, through training and inference, out to tool use and agent protocols. Ten arcs, eighty-odd posts.

A note on this series

I built this series as a learning project and used Claude extensively for research, drafting, critique, coding, and revision. I chose the curriculum and dependency order, drove the questions, edited the explanations, developed the interactive examples, and take responsibility for the published result.

This is an educational first edition rather than a formal reference. I am reviewing the articles against primary sources, testing the executable examples, and tracking corrections as the series matures. Some examples require specific hardware, model weights, or external services; they are teaching examples, not a tested deployment recipe.

The ten arcs

The ten arcs
  1. 01Vectors, Matrices, and the Spaces They Live In

    Vectors as lists of activations, matrix multiplication as a linear map, and how these operations fit into neural networks.

  2. 02Norms, Dot Products, and Similarity

    How vector length, dot products, cosine similarity, and projections support attention scores and embedding comparisons.

  3. 03Distributions, Softmax, and the Chain Rule of Words

    How softmax produces a categorical distribution, how Bayes' rule updates probabilities, and how the chain rule assigns probabilities to sequences.

  4. 04Cross-Entropy, KL Divergence, and What Loss Functions Measure

    Why cross-entropy is the standard LM loss, what it actually measures about two distributions, and how it connects to perplexity.

  5. 05Gradients and How Machines Learn

    What a gradient is, why it points uphill, how backpropagation computes one efficiently via the chain rule, and what SGD does with it.

  6. 06Optimizers: Momentum, Adam, and Learning Rate Schedules

    How momentum and Adam use gradient history, and how warmup and learning-rate schedules affect training.

  7. 07GPUs, Floating Point, and Why Precision Matters

    IEEE 754, the difference between fp32/fp16/bfloat16, why mixed-precision training works, and the basics of GPU parallelism.

  8. 08A Short Prehistory of Statistical NLP

    How rule-based, statistical, and early neural methods represented language and learned from text.
