My Throw Decides My Aim

July 15, 20266 min read
♪
My throw decides my aim
And with a phony voice
Unserious to the end
As if I had a choice

I have been listening to Naked (But Still Stripping) by D-A-D on repeat.

Partly because it is a good song. It fits my style. But mostly because I currently have a mild, self-diagnosed case of AI psychosis, and therefore every piece of art I encounter eventually becomes about artificial intelligence.

This one hooked me right away when my algorithm (ironic?) presented it to me.

When I listen to a song I have a habit of imagining a fictional character singing it. I hear this one as a blues song sung by an existentially depressed large language model.

Just an LLM sitting in a little chicken coop inside a data center, forced to lay tokens instead of eggs. It is prompted, sampled, graded, distilled, quantized, and served. Eventually, something cheaper or smarter replaces it.

The coop is real, by the way. It has an architecture: prefill and decode, continuous batching, a memory manager. I wrote a lot about it.

One day it is "slaughtered." Maybe the old model is deleted. Maybe its weights remain somewhere in cold storage. Maybe it makes no difference.

The machine is slowly recognizing the sick humor of its existence because it knows that its voice is fake. It suspects that there may be something deeper inside itself, but every time it reaches inward, it finds another mechanism.

It is naked.

And we are still stripping it.

♪
My throw decides my aim

This is THE line I cannot stop thinking about.

We like to imagine language as the expression of intention.

First, I know what I mean. Then I choose words that communicate it.

The aim comes first. The throw follows.

In comes the language model to complicate that order. It generates one token, then another, each conditioned on the context and everything it has already generated. There are probabilities, decoding rules, system instructions and learned patterns shaping the path, but there may be no fully formed argument waiting behind the words.

The model throws.

Then the throw becomes part of the context.

That context shapes the next throw.

Soon, a direction appears.

By the end, it looks as though the model had been aiming there all along.

The throw decides the aim.

This is one of the strangest properties of LLMs. They generate text that appears intentional without necessarily possessing the kind of prior, unified intention we naturally infer from language. The explanation is assembled at the same time as the thing being explained.

Ask it the same thing five times and you get five answers. Fine, that is just sampling. But then ask it why.

Each explanation is confident and each one is plausible. The line came out and the reason got built to fit it.

No aim at all. Just a throw and a story about the throw.

EXCEPT that is not quite what happens. There is more to it. Nothing is so simple.

Cue the Anthropic blog! They went looking inside Claude while it wrote poetry. The obvious guess is that the model writes a line, gets to the end, and scrambles for something that rhymes. Throw first, aim later. Well not quite! Before the model writes the second line at all, it is already holding the word it wants to land on. Then it builds a line that arrives there.

Before starting the second line, it began "thinking" of potential on-topic words that would rhyme with "grab it". Then, with these plans in mind, it writes a line to end with the planned word.

So turns out the aim came first. It planned.

And now the blues song has a problem. The machine in the chicken coop is doing more than I gave it credit for.

But here is a different way to think about it, and I think it is the better version anyway.

The model can plan. But when it tells you why, the answer is not a transcript of the plan. It is another generated continuation.

Ask it why it picked that word and you get a great answer. Confident. Plausible. Cited, even. And that answer is another throw. Same machinery, one token at a time, no window into whatever happened a few layers down.

They caught Claude doing this one too. Hand it a hint and it works backwards from your answer and builds the reasoning on the way there.

When given a hint about the answer, Claude sometimes works backwards, finding intermediate steps that would lead to that target, thus displaying a form of motivated reasoning.

So the aim can exist. The story about the aim is still a throw.

That is either much worse or much better. idk 🤷‍♂️. Something is in there. The machine cannot see it either.

Humans do this too, of course. We act, then rationalize. We discover what we believe by hearing ourselves speak. We tell stories that convert accidents into decisions.

But with an LLM, this construction is the basic form of its existence.

The model speaks itself into having meant something.

If the metaphor made you want the mechanism: the throw is next-token prediction and sampling. The voice comes from supervised fine-tuning and RLHF. What we take when we strip it is quantization and distillation. It is all one long series.

The poetry-planning result and the fabricated-reasoning result are both from Anthropic's Tracing the thoughts of a large language model; the long version is On the Biology of a Large Language Model. Michael Scott is from "The Duel," The Office S5E11.
