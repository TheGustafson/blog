Crosshair-Bench: Evaluating Frontier Models on an Unseen Game

October 28, 20264 min read

I wanted a board game to benchmark language models on that isn't sitting in their training data. So on October 25, 2026, I invented Crosshair and trained a tiny 114K-parameter AlphaZero net for it on my desktop GPU in six minutes to use as an anchor for LLM performance. None of these models have ever seen a Crosshair board or read a strategy guide for it. Should be safe for a few generations yet!

This note walks through the results from the Crosshair Benchmark.

How the tournament works

First, I built four engines in ai-crosshair to anchor the rating scale:

  • random-uct-50 (1078 Elo) — Monte Carlo Tree Search with 50 random rollouts per move and no neural network.
  • az-puct-1 (1200 Elo anchor) — The raw 114K-parameter AlphaZero policy network with tree search turned off (1 simulation, no lookahead).
  • az-puct-400 (1730 Elo) — The AlphaZero network with 400 PUCT tree-search simulations per move.
  • az-puct-4000 (2097 Elo) — The AlphaZero network with 4,000 PUCT tree-search simulations per move (0.28s per move).

Then I set up a 556-game tournament (crosshair-bench) across 20 language model configurations from OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, DeepSeek, and Qwen—each provider's Flagship model and smaller Flash model, tested at both high and low thinking budgets—plus the four engines above. Every matchup is 4 games with colors swapped. On each turn, the harness sends the rules, the 7×7 ASCII board, and the legal moves, while az-puct-4000 scores the move in the background (AZ-4000 Match in the table below). Check it out:

Against the engines

Every model played 4 games each against all four engines. The PUCT engines with larger simulation counts are much stronger than any language model: az-puct-4000 went 79–1 against the field and az-puct-400 went 77–3. Eighteen of the twenty models got swept 0–4 by both. Only gpt-6.1-sol@high (1–3 vs az-puct-4000, 2–2 vs az-puct-400) and gemini-4-argon@high (1–3 vs az-puct-400) managed to take games off them. Even when gpt-6.1-sol@high spent 85 seconds per move (~25 minutes and $0.63 per game) thinking through lines—roughly 300× slower per move than az-puct-4000 (0.28s per move)—az-puct-4000 still won 3 of the 4 games.

Without tree search, the top language models actually win. Against the raw policy network (az-puct-1, 1200 Elo), the models went 24–56 overall, with gpt-6.1-sol@high (1464 Elo) winning 3–1 head-to-head and gemini-4-argon@high (1410 Elo) and gpt-6.1-sol@low (1297 Elo) splitting 2–2 and finishing ahead of it on the rating scale. Against random-uct-50 (1078 Elo), the models split 40–40. When the neural net isn't allowed to search ahead, a frontier model thinking in its scratchpad can outplay it.

Thinking budget vs. model size

The results show that thinking budget is incredibly important. When matched up against itself with a higher thinking budget, the high-thinking configuration won 24–16 across the 10 models and rated +141 Elo higher on average.

It gets even clearer when you pit each provider's smaller Flash @high model against its own larger Flagship @low/@none model. Across those 20 games, the smaller models with high thinking budgets won 16–4:

On a game a model hasn't memorized, having tokens to actually work through moves matters way more than model size.

Flagship and Flash round-robins

I also ran two round-robins (4 games per pair): one among the five Flagship @high models, and one among the five Flash @high models.

In the Flagship @high round-robin, gemini-4-argon@high (14–2) and gpt-6.1-sol@high (13–3) dominated the other three models, splitting 2–2 with each other while Argon swept Opus, DeepSeek, and Qwen 4–0. Overall, gpt-6.1-sol@high (1464 Elo, 46.0% AZ-4000 Match) finished #1 among the language models ahead of gemini-4-argon@high (1410 Elo, 47.0% AZ-4000 Match, the highest among the language models) thanks to its stronger record against the AlphaZero engines.

In the Flash @high round-robin, gemini-3.8-flash@high went 13–3 (1157 Elo, 41.6% AZ-4000 Match) to take first ahead of gpt-6-luna@high (11–5, 1144 Elo), finishing with a higher Elo rating than three of the five Flagship @high models.
