Launching a better college football power index

November 8, 20262 min read

Today I'm launching a competitor to the other public college football ratings like ESPN's FPI and Bill Connelly's SP+, the CFB Power Index. In backtesting across 7,336 out-of-sample games since 2017, I found my index is more predictive than every other public power index out there. It beats ESPN's FPI, SP+, Pregame Elo, and FEI across the board on spread error (12.36 vs 12.54 FPI and 13.41 SP+ MAE), win probability log loss (0.4673 vs 0.4728 FPI and 0.5017 SP+), Brier score (0.1551), early-season Weeks 1–4 error (12.53 vs 14.25 SP+ MAE), and picking against the spread (52.1% overall, and 53.1% when it disagrees with the line by at least 3 points).

I'm able to beat them because I pull in more data and handle the messy parts of college football a bit more carefully. Before the season starts, the model builds priors for every position group from recruiting, returning production, transfer portal movement, and coaching track records across 144,000+ players. Once games start, it rates every team in points above an average FBS team (0.0) from 3.3 million plays across 19,008 games, filters out fourth-quarter garbage time and turnover luck, and automatically adjusts when a starting quarterback gets hurt or benched. Every team's rating splits cleanly into Offense + Defense + Special Teams (Pass, Rush, Finishing, and Havoc) and into Preseason Prior + In-Season Lift + QB Adjustment.

It is only narrowly beaten by the game-time market odds (12.36 vs 12.20 MAE, and basically tied at 0.4673 vs 0.4666 in log loss), which are able to price in practice-week injury reports, late opt-outs, weather, and sharp money right up until kickoff.
