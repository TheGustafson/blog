14:["$","h2",null,{"children":"Section 1: Introduction"}]
15:["$","p",null,{"children":"The paper opens with a clear argument: recurrent models are fundamentally sequential, and that sequentiality prevents parallelization."}]
16:["$","p",null,{"children":"In 2017, the state of the art for machine translation was RNNs augmented with attention. Bahdanau et al. (2015) had shown that you could let a decoder attend to encoder hidden states rather than compressing the entire source sentence into a single vector.[^6] Luong et al. refined the attention mechanism further. These models worked well. But they had a structural problem: because each hidden state depends on the previous one, you can't compute them in parallel. For long sequences on a GPU, that's a huge bottleneck."}]
17:["$","p",null,{"children":["If you've read ",["$","a",null,{"href":"/blog/seq2seq-and-bahdanau","children":"Post 2.7"}]," on seq2seq and Bahdanau attention, you've seen this world. The encoder processes the source sentence left to right, building up hidden states one at a time. The decoder does the same thing. Attention helped with the information bottleneck (the decoder could look back at all encoder states, not just the final one), but it didn't fix the sequential dependency. You still couldn't parallelize the recurrent computation."]}]
18:["$","p",null,{"children":"The transformer's argument was simple: replace the recurrence entirely. Use self-attention to let every position interact with every other position in a single operation. The entire sequence gets processed in one parallel step."}]
19:["$","p",null,{"children":"The paper skips Section 2 (just a brief background on existing attention mechanisms) and jumps to the architecture in Section 3. But before we go there, I want to note something about the introduction's framing. The authors position the transformer primarily as a speed improvement. \"The Transformer allows for significantly more parallelization and can reach a new state of the art in translation quality after being trained for as little as twelve hours on eight P100 GPUs.\" Twelve hours on eight GPUs. That was the pitch."}]
1a:["$","p",null,{"children":"What the paper didn't fully articulate, and maybe couldn't have known at the time, was that this parallelism argument went far beyond training speed. It turned out to be the key to scaling. When you scale a model from 100M to 100B parameters, you need to distribute computation across thousands of GPUs, and recurrent architectures are fundamentally hostile to that kind of parallelism in a way self-attention is not. So the transformer didn't only train faster. It was the only architecture that could actually exploit the hardware trajectory that was already underway. The authors were making a practical argument about wall-clock time on 2017 hardware, and the actual consequence was that they'd built the only architecture that could ride the compute scaling curve of the next decade."}]

25:["$","p",null,{"children":"The original transformer was designed to translate German to English.
