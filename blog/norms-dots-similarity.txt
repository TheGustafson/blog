69:["$","p",null,{"children":"A few things worth being precise about."}]
6a:["$","p",null,{"children":[["$","strong",null,{"children":"\"Cosine similarity measures semantic meaning.\""}]," Not exactly. Cosine similarity measures the geometric angle between two vectors. Whether that angle corresponds to semantic meaning depends entirely on how the embedding space was trained. If the training objective pushed similar words to have similar vectors (like Word2Vec or a sentence transformer did), then yes, a high cosine similarity correlates with semantic relatedness. But cosine similarity itself has no idea what the vectors mean. You could compute cosine similarity between two random vectors and get a number. It just wouldn't mean anything semantically. The geometry is meaningful only because the training process made it so.[^2]"]}]
6b:["$","p",null,{"children":[["$","strong",null,{"children":"\"A bigger dot product always means more similar.\""}]," Only if the vectors have the same magnitude. 6c:["$","p",null,{"children":[["$","strong",null,{"children":"\"Euclidean distance and cosine similarity always agree.\""}]," They don't, at least not for unnormalized vectors.
