Crosshair LLM Benchmark

Wouldn't it be nice to have a board game to evaluate models against that isn't in the training data? Well, I introduce to you Crosshair, which I invented on October 25, 2026. Should be safe for a few generations yet!

Crosshair is a 7×7 connection-and-flip board game built specifically to work well for a benchmark like this:

  • Zero training contamination: Crosshair didn't exist when these models were trained, so no games, opening books, or strategy guides appear in pretraining data. Every move has to be worked out from the four-sentence ruleset in the prompt.
  • No draws and fast games: Red connects top to bottom; Blue connects left to right. Orthogonal sandwiches and 2×2 diagonal crosscuts flip enemy stones in place and can chain across the board, so draws cannot happen and every game ends by ply 49 (usually in 25 to 40 plies).
  • Small branching factor (~8 to 12 legal moves per turn): Playing at (c, r) restricts your opponent's next move to empty squares in Row r or Column c (unless that cross is full, which gives them a free move anywhere). That keeps the move list short enough for a model to actually work through in its scratchpad, while still leaving plenty of room for 3- to 5-ply traps.

Plus, I trained a few Monte Carlo Tree Search and AlphaZero engines in Rust (ai-crosshair) to give us a baseline for engine strength. The neural network is only 114K parameters and trained in six minutes on my desktop GPU, which makes it a fun yardstick for frontier language models:

random-uct-501135 Elo
50 uniform rollouts · 8 ms/move

Plain MCTS with random playouts and no neural network or hand-written evaluation heuristics.

az-puct-11200 Elo (Anchor)
1 simulation · 0.04 ms/move

The raw 114K-parameter (457 KB) AlphaZero network with tree search turned off. Picks its top policy move with zero lookahead.

az-puct-4001888 Elo
400 PUCT sims · 26 ms/move

The 114K network guiding a 400-simulation PUCT tree. Also grades every turn in the tournament as our shadow oracle.

az-puct-40002343 Elo
4,000 PUCT sims · 279 ms/move

Deeper 4,000-simulation AlphaZero search. Went 80–0 across all 20 frontier and flash LLM configurations.

How the harness works: On every turn, the active model receives a stateless prompt containing the four-sentence ruleset, the 7×7 ASCII board, coordinate lists for Red and Blue stones, the active row/column crosshair constraint, and the explicit list of legal moves. Every matchup is played in color-swapped pairs from balanced 2-ply openings, and az-puct-400 evaluates every position before and after the chosen move to measure Top-1 move agreement, mean win-probability loss (MWPL), and blunder rate (moves losing at least 10% win probability).
